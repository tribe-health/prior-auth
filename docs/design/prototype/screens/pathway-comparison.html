Step 05 · Pathway comparison

Three ways to read this chart.
Two of them will be denied.

The system proposes and ranks. It does not choose. Each pathway states what it requires the operative plan to be, so the surgeon can see the commitment before the prose exists.

What the ranking optimizes for

Approval likelihood given the documented findings and the surgeon's genuine prospective plan — not the most complete description of the patient's disease. A pathway is only viable if the chart already supports it and the planned operation actually matches it.

Ranked 01Recommended

Iatrogenic instability from planned facetectomy

SURG-2026-014 rev. 4 · §4.1(d), second clause · p.11–12

The decompression required to relieve the foraminal stenosis necessitates resecting more than half of the right L4–L5 facet. The policy treats that resection as itself establishing the instability that justifies fusion — no radiographic threshold needed.

Operative assumption this pathway requires

That the facetectomy is genuinely planned and genuinely exceeds 50% of the joint. If the surgeon intends a facet-sparing foraminotomy, this pathway collapses and must not be selected.

Why this over the alternatives

It routes around the two weakest facts in the chart. The therapy shortfall is answered by the policy's own non-progression clause rather than argued, and the 6 mm translation — which a reviewer could dispute on measurement technique — becomes corroboration instead of the load-bearing element.

Operative assumption this pathway requires

That the surgeon will perform decompression without fusion. That is not the prospective plan, so selecting this pathway would misdescribe the operation.

Recorded, not hidden

It appears here because a coordinator under deadline pressure will find §3.2 and notice it is easier to satisfy. Naming why it fails is more useful than omitting it.

One gap is common to both viable pathways

Nicotine status blocks §4.1(e) under either fusion theory. A cotinine result is worth more to this case than any argument the letter could make — it is one lab draw, and it is assigned to Nathan, PA-C.

Recommended combination

Primary · Pathway 01

Iatrogenic instability from the planned facetectomy.

Supporting · Pathway 02

The 6 mm translation cited as corroboration of the same conclusion — not as the element the approval rests on.

A supporting pathway strengthens the letter only while the primary theory stays intact. The drafting engine will not let the prose drift between the two.

What is not on this board

No central stenosis pathway, no motor-deficit pathway, no multi-level deformity pathway. Each would require a predicate this chart does not contain, and the system will not create one to open a route.
