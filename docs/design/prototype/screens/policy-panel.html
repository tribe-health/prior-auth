§ 3.2 · p.7

Decompression without fusion

Laminectomy, laminotomy, or foraminotomy is considered medically necessary when radiculopathy or neurogenic claudication is present and corroborated by advanced imaging, and when at least six weeks of supervised conservative therapy has been completed or is contraindicated.

§ 4.1 · p.11–12 · governing section for this request

Decompression with instrumented fusion — iatrogenic or documented instability

Single-level instrumented posterior fusion is considered medically necessary when all of the following are documented:
  1. a. Radicular pain or neurogenic claudication of at least six months' duration that has not responded to conservative management;
  2. b. Advanced imaging demonstrating nerve root compression at the level to be treated, corroborated by physical examination findings referable to that level;
  3. c. At least six weeks of supervised physical therapy, or documentation that such therapy is contraindicated or was discontinued for non-progression;
  4. d. Segmental instability demonstrated by ≥ 4 mm translation or ≥ 10° angular motion on flexion-extension radiographs, or a planned decompression that requires resection of more than 50% of a facet joint at the operative level;
  5. e. Absence of active nicotine use, or a documented cessation program, where interbody fusion is planned.

§ 4.2 · p.13

Multi-level fusion and deformity

Fusion of two or more contiguous levels requires the criteria in §4.1 at each level, plus coronal or sagittal deformity meeting the thresholds in Table 4.2-A. Not applicable to a single-level request.

§ 4.2(c) · p.14

Preoperative optimization

Where interbody device placement is planned, documented nicotine abstinence and an HbA1c below 8.0% within ninety days are required. Bone-density assessment is required only where pelvic fixation is planned.

§ 6.4 · p.22

Criteria disclosure and reviewer qualification

Upon adverse determination the plan will provide, on request, the specific criteria and the version applied, and the specialty of the reviewing physician.
